AI safe-use checklist

Eleven practical checks to help your team use AI tools like ChatGPT, Copilot and Gemini without putting customer or company data at risk. Print it, share it, tick it off.

How I help small businesses

Tools

  1. Keep a short list of approved AI tools.Write down which tools your team may use for work, and for what. Anything not on the list gets checked before anyone uses it.
  2. Use business accounts, not personal free ones.Business and team plans usually give you more control over your data and let you remove access when someone leaves.
  3. Check each tool’s data settings.Where a tool allows it, turn off the option that lets it use your conversations to train its models, and review how long it keeps your data.

Data

  1. Decide what never goes into AI.Customer personal details, passwords, payment information, health information and confidential contracts stay out of any tool not approved for them.
  2. Clean up file sharing before turning on AI assistants.Microsoft Copilot and Google Gemini can search everything a person can open. Fix overshared folders and “anyone with the link” files first.
  3. Remove sensitive details when you can.When you do need help with a real document, swap names, account numbers and addresses for placeholders first.

People

  1. A person checks AI output before it reaches a customer.AI can sound confident and still be wrong. Someone reviews facts, numbers and tone before anything goes out.
  2. Be open about AI with customers.If customers talk to an AI chat assistant, tell them, and give them an easy way to reach a person.
  3. Give your team a short training.Thirty minutes on your rules, good prompts and when not to use AI prevents most mistakes.

Ongoing

  1. Name one person responsible for your AI rules.Someone owns the approved-tools list and the policy, and is the person staff ask when unsure.
  2. Review everything every 3 to 6 months.AI tools and their settings change often. A quick review keeps your list, settings and rules current.

This checklist is general guidance, not legal advice. Businesses that handle health, financial or children’s data may have extra rules to follow.
